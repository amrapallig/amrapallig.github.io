Amrapalli Garanaik, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor (Senior Research)
College of Earth, Ocean, and Atmospheric Sciences · Oregon State University

I am a fluid dynamicist and physical oceanographer studying the physics of small-scale turbulence and mixing in geophysical and environmental flows. I combine Earth system modeling, direct numerical simulation (DNS) and large eddy simulation (LES) with theory and observations to understand multi-scale interactions, and I develop subgrid-scale parameterizations that improve how climate models represent ocean mixing.

What I work on

Research

My work bridges scales, from centimeter-scale turbulence in the lab and in DNS to global ocean models, with the goal of building physically grounded mixing parameterizations for climate prediction.

Earth system modeling

A new boundary layer scheme for Earth system models

We developed a physically motivated mixing scheme for ocean surface boundary layer (OSBL) turbulence and implemented it in MPAS-Ocean, the ocean component of the DOE's Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM). The scheme captures OSBL physics driven by buoyancy, wind and wave forcing efficiently, which makes it a promising way to reduce biases in general circulation models (GCMs).

DNS · Theory

Stratified turbulence through direct numerical simulation

DNS of stably stratified turbulence reveals flow dynamics and mixing. It provides physics-based parameterizations of diapycnal mixing and ways to infer them from quantities that can be measured in the field.

JFM 2019 Phys. Fluids 2022 Phys. Fluids 2018
Observations

Air–sea interaction from satellite and in-situ data

As a member of the air–sea interaction and velocity working groups in NASA's Sub-Mesoscale Ocean Dynamics Experiment (S-MODE), I study surface flux estimates and their response to SST and ocean currents to improve coupled ocean–atmosphere models. I also took part in the ASIRI project studying subsurface mixing in the Bay of Bengal.

HPC · LES

Large eddy simulations of the ocean on GPUs

We run ocean LES with Oceananigans.jl on NVIDIA A100, V100 and H100 GPUs and on Grace Hopper systems at Oregon State University, which opens the way to high-resolution turbulence simulations.

OSM 2024 abstract →
Mesoscale · Lagrangian

Mesoscale mixing from Lagrangian particles

Using the LIGHT particle-tracking package (Lagrangian, In situ, Global, High-performance particle Tracking) in eddy-resolving MPAS-Ocean (E3SM), we quantify horizontal and vertical variability of isopycnal diffusivity to develop better parameterizations.

Laboratory

Experimental fluid mechanics

We measured turbulent open-channel flows with Acoustic Doppler Velocimetry (ADV) and Laser Doppler Anemometry (LDA) in a recirculating flume at Colorado State University, and the results show how the two sensors compare in practice.
