Amrapalli Garanaik, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor (Senior Research) · Physical Oceanographer · Fluid Dynamicist
College of Earth, Ocean, and Atmospheric Sciences · Oregon State University

I study the fluid mechanics of turbulent mixing, transport, and multiscale interactions in geophysical and environmental flows. My work combines theory, high-resolution simulations (DNS and LES), Earth system modeling, observations, and GPU-accelerated computing with physics-informed machine learning. The aim is to understand processes too small for climate models to resolve and to represent them better in large-scale Earth system models.

What I work on

Research

I study turbulence and mixing across scales, from centimeter-scale eddies in the lab and in direct numerical simulations to global ocean models. The goal is to build physically grounded parameterizations that improve climate prediction.

  • Upper-ocean dynamics
  • Vertical mixing parameterizations
  • Earth system modeling
  • LES & GPU computing
  • Stratified turbulence
  • Particle tracking
  • Ice–ocean interactions
  • Laboratory experiments

Upper-ocean dynamics & air–sea interaction

How wind, surface waves, solar heating and buoyancy forcing drive turbulence in the ocean surface boundary layer, and how that turbulence controls the exchange of heat and momentum with the atmosphere.

Current work

  • Diurnal warm layers under variable winds and surface waves, combining simulations with observations from Buzzards Bay. With S. Zippel, T. Kukulka, M. Scully, F. C. Yeh
  • Air–sea heat and momentum flux response to SST and surface-current variability from Saildrone observations in NASA's S-MODE experiment. With R. Samelson, L. O'Neill Manuscript in prep
  • Coupling of the tropical air–sea boundary layers through observational analysis and multiscale coupled modeling.
  • Surface waves and vertical mixing in wintertime sea-ice leads. With A. Lee, J. Hutchings, B. Pearson Manuscript in prep

Past work

  • Subsurface mixing and upper-ocean temperature structure in the Bay of Bengal (ASIRI), including uCTD fieldwork aboard R/V Roger Revelle, 2014. Oceanography 2016

New vertical mixing parameterizations

Physically based turbulence closures that capture the non-local, non-diffusive mixing done by convective plumes and Langmuir cells, which standard eddy-diffusivity schemes miss.

Current work

  • A plume-based, non-local turbulence closure for realistic, time-varying and non-equilibrium forcing, built to run on GPUs. APS DFD 2025
  • Nonlocal second-moment modeling of the upper-ocean boundary layer, constrained by large eddy simulations and observations.
  • An assumed-distribution, high-order closure for wave-driven Langmuir turbulence. Manuscript in prep

Past work

  • A hybrid mass-flux/high-order turbulence closure for ocean vertical mixing. JAMES 2024 APS DFD 2023
  • A plume-based high-order closure that captures convective and Langmuir mixing. APS DFD 2024
  • Evaluation of the standard k–ε closure for stably stratified wall-bounded turbulence. Manuscript in prep

Earth system modeling

Implementing and testing new ocean physics in the U.S. Department of Energy's Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM) and its ocean component, MPAS-Ocean, to reduce biases in climate simulations.

Current work

  • Implementing and evaluating plume-based hybrid mass-flux/high-order closures in E3SM/MPAS-Ocean.
  • Wildfire and regional coupled modeling on Oregon State University's Huang Complex supercomputer.

Past work

  • Developed and implemented a new boundary layer scheme in MPAS-Ocean at Los Alamos National Laboratory (2019–2021). JAMES 2024
  • Online Lagrangian particles in eddy-resolving MPAS-Ocean simulations on NERSC supercomputers (see Particle tracking).

Large eddy simulation & GPU computing

High-resolution large eddy simulations (LES) of ocean turbulence on modern GPU hardware, used as the benchmark for building and testing parameterizations.

Current work

  • GPU-accelerated LES with Oceananigans.jl on NVIDIA A100, V100 and H100 GPUs and Grace Hopper systems at OSU. OSM 2024
  • LES of Langmuir turbulence and wave- and wind-driven mixing to study wave–current interaction and vertical transport.

Past work

  • LES of convective and Langmuir turbulence in the surface boundary layer, used to validate new mixing schemes. JAMES 2024

Stratified turbulence & mixing

Direct numerical simulation (DNS) and theory that show how stable stratification controls turbulence, mixing efficiency and diapycnal diffusivity, and how mixing can be inferred from quantities measured in the field.

Past work

  • New insights for inferring diapycnal (irreversible) diffusivity in stably stratified turbulence. Phys. Fluids 2022
  • Inferring the state of turbulence and mixing efficiency in stably stratified flows. J. Fluid Mech. 2019
  • Small-scale anisotropy in stably stratified turbulence from DNS. Phys. Fluids 2018

Lagrangian particle tracking & mesoscale mixing

Following water parcels through turbulent and eddying flows to measure transport, dispersion and mixing directly.

Current work

  • GPU-enabled Lagrangian particle tracking in high-resolution LES, to study particle transport, dispersion and non-local turbulent mixing.

Past work

  • Horizontal and vertical variability of isopycnal diffusivity from online Lagrangian particles (the LIGHT package) in eddy-resolving MPAS-Ocean. With M. R. Petersen, P. J. Wolfram Manuscript in prep

Ice–ocean interactions

How ocean turbulence and waves near ice affect melting and mixing at polar boundaries.

Current work

  • Exploratory simulations of internal waves generated by glacier calving and subglacial discharge, and their role in submarine melting at Greenland's marine-terminating glaciers. With J. Cusack, K. Zhao OSM 2026 · AGU 2025
  • Turbulent mixing in wintertime sea-ice leads (see Upper-ocean dynamics).

Laboratory experiments

Measuring turbulence in the lab to test instruments and theory.

Past work

  • Turbulent open-channel flow measured with Acoustic Doppler Velocimetry (ADV) and Laser Doppler Anemometry (LDA) on a moving traverse at Colorado State University. Flow Meas. Instrum. 2022
  • Rotating-table and salt-fingering experiments (see Fun Projects).

Emerging directions

  • Physics-informed machine learning for high-order turbulent fluxes
