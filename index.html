Amrapalli Garanaik, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor (Senior Research) · Physical Oceanographer · Fluid Dynamicist
College of Earth, Ocean, and Atmospheric Sciences · Oregon State University

I study the fluid mechanics of turbulent mixing, transport, and multiscale interactions in geophysical and environmental flows. My work combines theory, high-resolution simulations (DNS and LES), Earth system modeling, observations, and GPU-accelerated computing with physics-informed machine learning. The aim is to understand processes too small for climate models to resolve and to represent them better in large-scale Earth system models.

What I work on

Research

I study turbulence and mixing across scales, from centimeter-scale eddies in the lab and in direct numerical simulations to global ocean models. The goal is to build physically grounded parameterizations that improve climate prediction.

Upper-ocean mixing parameterization

New physically based closures, including hybrid mass-flux/high-order and plume-based non-local schemes, that capture mixing by convective plumes and Langmuir cells, which standard eddy-diffusivity schemes miss.

JAMES 2024 APS DFD 2025 APS DFD 2024 APS DFD 2023

Langmuir turbulence

How wind and surface waves together drive counter-rotating Langmuir cells that deepen the mixed layer. We study this with GPU-accelerated LES and second-moment closures, including in wintertime sea-ice leads.

Video: vertical velocity (w) in a 0.5 m-resolution LES of Langmuir turbulence. Streaks at the surface mark the Langmuir cells, and blue plumes carry water downward.

APS DFD 2024 Manuscripts in prep

Particle tracking in the ocean surface boundary layer

GPU-enabled Lagrangian particle tracking inside high-resolution LES, used to follow how Langmuir turbulence and convection transport, disperse and mix water parcels, including non-local transport. Part of this work was done with 2025 NSF REU students.

Video: 2,048 particles in an LES of Langmuir turbulence, colored by vertical displacement Δz.

Diurnal warm layers

How daytime solar heating, variable winds and surface waves interact to form and break down diurnal warm layers, and what that means for upper-ocean turbulence and mixing. This work combines simulations with observations from Buzzards Bay. With F. C. Yeh, M. Scully, T. Kukulka, S. Zippel

OSM 2026 poster Manuscript in prep

Stratified turbulence

Direct numerical simulation (DNS) and theory that show how stable stratification controls turbulence, mixing efficiency and diapycnal diffusivity, and how mixing can be inferred from quantities measured in the field.

Phys. Fluids 2022 J. Fluid Mech. 2019 Phys. Fluids 2018

Earth system modeling

Implementing and testing new ocean physics in the DOE's Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM) and its ocean component, MPAS-Ocean, to reduce biases in climate simulations.

JAMES 2024

Mesoscale mixing

Online Lagrangian particles (the LIGHT package) in eddy-resolving MPAS-Ocean, used to quantify horizontal and vertical variability of isopycnal diffusivity. We are now extending particle tracking to GPU-based LES.

Manuscript in prep

Air–sea interaction

How SST, surface currents, diurnal warm layers and waves shape the exchange of heat and momentum between ocean and atmosphere. This work uses Saildrone data from NASA's S-MODE, observations from Buzzards Bay, and tropical coupled modeling. I also did fieldwork on the ASIRI cruise in the Bay of Bengal.

Oceanography 2016 S-MODE manuscript in prep

GPU computing

Our lab has an NVIDIA DGX system for GPU-accelerated ocean simulations. We run LES with Oceananigans.jl on DGX, A100, V100 and H100 GPUs and on Grace Hopper systems at OSU. The benchmark (right) compares wall time on CPUs and GPUs as the grid grows.

Ice–ocean interaction

Simulations of internal waves generated by glacier calving and subglacial discharge, and their effect on submarine melting at Greenland's marine-terminating glaciers.

OSM 2026 · AGU 2025

Laboratory experiments

We measured turbulent open-channel flows with ADV and LDA on a moving traverse at Colorado State University, and I have run rotating-table and salt-fingering experiments.

Flow Meas. Instrum. 2022 Fun projects

Physics-informed AI

Combining physics-informed machine learning and data-driven analysis to improve parameterizations of high-order turbulent fluxes in ocean and Earth system models.
